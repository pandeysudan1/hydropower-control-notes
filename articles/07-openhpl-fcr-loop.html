07 — Building the Complete OpenHPL FCR Loop

The complete primary-frequency-control model combines the acausal hydropower plant with the signal-based governor.

1. Closed-loop chain

$$\Delta f\to\text{Governor}\to\Delta y_g\to\text{Francis}\to\Delta P_m\to\text{Generator/Grid}\to\Delta f.$$

The waterway sits inside the turbine-power path, so the plant seen by the governor is dynamic.

2. Physical plant

$$F_p(\dot x_p,x_p,z_p,y_g,P_L)=0.$$

Here $x_p$ includes reservoir, penstock, surge-tank, turbine/shaft and generator dynamic states, while $z_p$ contains algebraic pressure, flow, torque and electrical-network variables.

3. Governor

$$\dot x_c=f_c(x_c,f,P_{ref}),\qquad y_g=h_c(x_c,f,P_{ref}).$$

4. Disturbance

A load increase is modeled by

$$P_L=P_{L0}+\Delta P_L H(t-t_d).$$

Immediately after the disturbance, mechanical power has not changed, so

$$\Delta P_m-\Delta P_e<0,$$

and frequency falls.

5. Primary response

The governor sees $\Delta f<0$ and increases guide-vane opening. The resulting power increment can be represented as

$$\Delta P_m(s)=G_h(s)G_t(s)G_g(s)\Delta f(s),$$

where $G_g$ is the governor/servo, $G_h$ the waterway dynamics, and $G_t$ the turbine conversion.

6. Frequency equation

A reduced generator-grid model is

$$M\Delta\dot\omega+D\Delta\omega=\Delta P_m-\Delta P_L.$$

Thus

$$\Delta\omega(s)=\frac{\Delta P_m(s)-\Delta P_L(s)}{Ms+D}.$$

7. Droop equilibrium

With permanent droop and no secondary control, steady-state frequency error is generally nonzero. If

$$\Delta P_m=-\frac{1}{R}\Delta f,$$

then for a single-area approximation

$$\Delta f_{ss}=-\frac{\Delta P_L}{D+1/R}.$$

8. FCR performance quantities

Useful outputs are nadir, RoCoF, settling time, delivered power, gate travel, pressure excursion and surge-tank level. A hydropower-specific qualification study should therefore inspect both electrical and hydraulic constraints.

9. Why OpenHPL is valuable

A textbook governor-turbine block can match low-frequency behavior, but OpenHPL exposes whether the same control action causes unacceptable pressure waves, surge oscillations, guide-vane saturation or turbine operating-point excursions.
