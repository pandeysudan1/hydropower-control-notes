04 — Francis Turbine: Hydraulic-to-Mechanical Coupling

OpenHPL provides several turbine models. The Francis component is especially useful for frequency-control studies because it explicitly couples hydraulic flow, pressure, guide-vane geometry, runner kinematics, shaft speed and losses.

1. Inputs and outputs

The turbine receives hydraulic inlet/outlet conditions through physical connectors, a guide-vane control signal, and generator angular velocity. It produces shaft power and hydraulic outflow.

2. Flow and pressure

Mass flow and volume flow are related by

$$Q=\frac{\dot m}{\rho},$$

or, when compressibility is enabled, by a pressure-dependent density. The turbine pressure drop is determined from the runner and optional guide-vane losses.

3. Euler turbine power

The key mechanical conversion is based on the Euler turbine equation. OpenHPL computes two power terms associated with inlet and outlet velocity triangles. In compact notation,

$$P_{Euler}=\dot m\,\omega\left(R_1c_{u1}-R_2c_{u2}\right).$$

In the implementation these terms are constructed from runner radius, angular speed, volume flow, cross-sectional areas and blade/guide-vane angles.

4. Loss model

The model separates shock, whirl and wall-friction losses:

$$P_{loss}=P_{shock}+P_{whirl}+P_{friction}.$$

A simplified shaft-power expression is therefore

$$P_m=P_{Euler}-P_{loss}.$$

At low load, OpenHPL switches to a stronger loss term to regularize and balance the turbine model.

5. Guide-vane geometry

The guide-vane command does not merely multiply flow. The servo position is converted through linkage geometry into guide-vane angle $\alpha_1$, which changes the inlet velocity triangle and therefore both flow and torque. Symbolically,

$$u_t\to Y\to\theta\to\alpha_1\to c_{u1}\to P_m.$$

This is one reason the nonlinear OpenHPL turbine is richer than the textbook relation $P_m=\eta\rho gQH$.

6. Useful reduced relation

A control-oriented approximation around an operating point $(Q^\star,H^\star,\omega^\star,y_g^\star)$ is

$$\Delta P_m\approx K_Q\Delta Q+K_H\Delta H+K_\omega\Delta\omega+K_y\Delta y_g,$$

where the coefficients are Jacobian terms evaluated from the nonlinear turbine model.

7. Torque coupling

The shaft torque is

$$T_m=\frac{P_m}{\omega},$$

so the turbine is the constitutive bridge between the hydraulic DAE and the generator rotational dynamics.

8. FCR meaning

The FCR loop acts primarily through $\Delta y_g$. Because the turbine relation depends on hydraulic states and speed, the effective gain

$$\frac{\Delta P_m}{\Delta y_g}$$

is frequency dependent once coupled to the waterway. This is the transfer function we ultimately want to extract from the OpenHPL operating point.
