09 — Continuous Physics, Digital Events and Homotopy

The nonlinear OpenHPL plant is naturally continuous-time, while practical supervisory control may be sampled or event driven. These ideas should not be confused with acausal modeling.

1. Continuous plant

$$F(\dot x,x,z,u)=0.$$

Between controller events, $u$ may be held constant or generated by continuous controller states.

2. Digital controller

For sampling period $T_s$,

$$t_k=kT_s,$$ $$q_{k+1}=\Phi(q_k,y(t_k)),$$

and with zero-order hold

$$u(t)=u_k,\qquad t_k\le t3. Multi-rate control

A fast local governor and slower AGC can have different clocks:

$$t_k^{(g)}=kT_g,\qquad t_j^{(AGC)}=jT_A,$$

with $T_g\ll T_A$.

4. Event consistency

At a discrete controller update, the continuous states often remain continuous,

$$x^+=x^-,$$

but algebraic variables may need re-solving:

$$g(x^-,z^+,u^+)=0.$$

5. Why execution order matters

If two discrete controllers update at the same event time, their maps need not commute:

$$\Phi_1(\Phi_2(q))\neq\Phi_2(\Phi_1(q)).$$

That is an execution-order issue. It is not evidence that the underlying physical model must be causal.

6. Homotopy

When a nonlinear algebraic problem is difficult, a continuation problem can be introduced:

$$H(z,\lambda)=(1-\lambda)g_0(z)+\lambda g(z)=0,$$

with

$$\lambda:0\to1.$$

Differentiating the continuation path gives

$$\frac{dz}{d\lambda}=-H_z^{-1}H_\lambda.$$

7. Physical meaning

The continuation coordinate is numerical, not physical:

$$\boxed{t\neq k\neq\lambda}.$$

$t$ is physical time, $k$ is a controller-event index, and $\lambda$ is a numerical continuation coordinate.

8. Practical implication

For OpenHPL-based FCR studies, the clean separation is:

$$\text{continuous acausal plant}+\text{continuous/discrete controller}+\text{numerical solver machinery}.$$

This distinction makes it much easier to reason about Modelica simulation, digital implementation and later Julia-based control analysis.
