05 — OpenHPL Generator and Grid Coupling

OpenHPL includes both SimpleGen and SynchGen. For learning FCR, the simple generator is a useful starting point because it exposes the essential rotational energy balance without burying it inside electromagnetic states.

1. Rotating kinetic energy

$$K=\frac12J\omega^2.$$

Differentiating gives

$$\frac{dK}{dt}=J\omega\dot\omega.$$

2. Power balance

OpenHPL documents the simple generator through

$$\frac{dK}{dt}=P_s-P_f-P_g,$$

where $P_s$ is turbine shaft power, $P_f$ is mechanical friction loss, and $P_g$ is the mechanical power converted by the generator.

Hence, for $\omega\neq0$,

$$J\dot\omega=\frac{P_s-P_f-P_g}{\omega}.$$

3. Friction

A simple bearing-loss model is

$$P_f=\frac12k_f\omega^2.$$

4. Electrical output

With electrical efficiency $\eta_e$,

$$P_e=\eta_e P_g.$$

This model can be loaded directly by an electrical power-demand input or coupled mechanically/electrically to a grid model.

5. Frequency

For a synchronous machine with $p$ pole pairs, mechanical speed and electrical frequency satisfy

$$\omega_m=\frac{2\pi f}{p},$$

so speed deviations map directly to frequency deviations.

6. Swing-equation form

After choosing nominal speed $\omega_0$ and linearizing, the generator balance can be written in familiar power-system form:

$$M\Delta\dot\omega=\Delta P_m-\Delta P_e-D\Delta\omega.$$

This is not a separate physical law; it is a reduced representation of the same rotational energy balance.

7. More detailed synchronous generator

The SynchGen component introduces more complete synchronous-machine behavior. In that case, electrical states and algebraic network constraints become part of the complete DAE:

$$F_g(\dot x_g,x_g,z_g,T_m,v,i)=0.$$

8. Grid coupling

When the machine is connected to a larger power system, electrical power becomes a network-dependent quantity:

$$P_e=P_e(\delta,V,E,X,\ldots).$$

The hydropower unit then participates in a closed electromechanical loop:

$$P_m\to\omega\to f\to P_e,$$

with governor feedback returning from frequency to the turbine guide vane.

9. Disturbance for LFC/FCR

A load step can be represented by

$$P_L(t)=P_{L0}+\Delta P_L H(t-t_d),$$

which initially creates

$$\Delta P_m-\Delta P_e<0.$$

The rotor decelerates, frequency falls, and the governor responds by opening the guide vanes.
