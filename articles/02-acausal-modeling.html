02 — Acausal Modeling and OpenHPL Interfaces

OpenHPL is naturally acausal because physical components are connected by conservation variables rather than by a fixed signal-flow direction.

1. Hydraulic connection

A hydraulic interface can be understood through effort and flow variables. Pressure is an effort-like variable and mass flow is a flow-like variable. At a connection node,

$$p_1=p_2,$$

while conservation gives

$$\dot m_1+\dot m_2=0.$$

No component is declared to be the permanent input or output. The solver determines the computational causality from the assembled equations.

2. Mechanical connection

For rotational components, angular velocity is shared and torques balance:

$$\omega_1=\omega_2,$$ $$\tau_1+\tau_2=0.$$

This is how the hydraulic turbine and generator can be connected through a shaft without manually imposing a one-way signal path.

3. Electrical connection

The same idea applies to electric networks:

$$v_1=v_2,\qquad i_1+i_2=0.$$

OpenHPL can therefore be coupled to power-system models while preserving the equation-based formulation.

4. Causal versus acausal mathematics

A causal block usually has the explicit form

$$y=\phi(u).$$

An acausal component is more naturally written as

$$F(y,u)=0.$$

After many components are connected, the full system becomes

$$F(\dot x,x,z,u)=0.$$

The numerical tool then performs structural analysis, index reduction if needed, and chooses a solvable representation.

5. OpenHPL examples

Reservoir, PenstockKP, SurgeTank, Francis, and generator components are connected by physical interfaces. Their internal equations contain mass balances, momentum balances, constitutive pressure/flow relations, torque balances, and energy balances.

6. Controller blocks are different

The governor is signal-oriented rather than a physical conservation component. It uses frequency and power-reference inputs and produces a guide-vane command. This does not make the plant causal; it only means the controller itself is conveniently represented as a directed signal system.

The complete closed loop is therefore a mixed formulation:

$$\underbrace{F_p(\dot x,x,z,y_g)=0}_{\text{acausal physical plant}},$$ $$\underbrace{\dot x_c=f_c(x_c,f,P_{ref}),\quad y_g=h_c(x_c,f,P_{ref})}_{\text{controller}}.$$

7. Why this matters for FCR

For control analysis we may later expose selected inputs and outputs, for example

$$u_c=\Delta y_g,\qquad y_c=\Delta f,$$

but that causal representation is a reduction of the acausal physical model around an operating point, not the original modeling assumption.

8. Key distinction

$$\boxed{\text{acausal/causal modeling}\neq\text{synchronous/asynchronous execution}}$$

Acausality is about how physical equations are posed. Synchronization is about how numerical or digital events are scheduled. These concepts should remain separate throughout the series.
