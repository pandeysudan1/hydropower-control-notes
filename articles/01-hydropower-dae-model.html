Hydropower Control Notes

Nonlinear Hydropower DAE Model

This note develops a compact nonlinear differential-algebraic model of a hydropower plant, starting from the reservoir and waterways and ending at the turbine, shaft, generator, electrical network, and load. The objective is to obtain a physically interpretable nonlinear model that can later be linearized around an operating point for load-frequency control and FCR studies.

1. Physical system and assumptions

We consider the chain

\[\text{reservoir}\rightarrow\text{intake conduit}\rightarrow\text{surge tank}\rightarrow\text{penstock}\rightarrow\text{turbine}\rightarrow\text{shaft}\rightarrow\text{generator}\rightarrow\text{grid/load}.\]

The reservoir and tailrace are treated as constant-head boundaries, the waterways are represented by lumped momentum equations with nonlinear friction, the surge tank introduces storage, the turbine is algebraic, the shaft provides rotational dynamics, and the simplified generator/network model is algebraic.

2. Differential and algebraic variables

A convenient differential-state vector is

\[x=\begin{bmatrix}Q_1 & h_s & Q_2 & \delta & \omega\end{bmatrix}^{T}.\]

Here, \(Q_1\) is the upstream conduit flow, \(h_s\) the surge-tank level, \(Q_2\) the downstream penstock/turbine flow, \(\delta\) the rotor angle, and \(\omega\) the angular speed.

A possible algebraic vector is

\[z=\begin{bmatrix}H_t & P_h & P_m & T_m & P_e & P_L\end{bmatrix}^{T},\]

with external or control inputs

\[u=\begin{bmatrix}y_g & H_r & H_{tr} & P_{\mathrm{grid}}\end{bmatrix}^{T}.\]

3. Reservoir and tailrace boundaries

\[H_r=H_{r0},\qquad H_{tr}=H_{tr0}.\]

These are algebraic boundary conditions and introduce no dynamic states.

4. Upstream conduit dynamics

For a lumped water column, the momentum balance is

\[L_1\dot Q_1=gA_1\left(H_r-h_s-h_{f,1}(Q_1)\right).\]

With quadratic friction,

\[h_{f,1}(Q_1)=K_1Q_1|Q_1|,\]

so

\[\boxed{\dot Q_1=\frac{gA_1}{L_1}\left(H_r-h_s-K_1Q_1|Q_1|\right)}.\]

5. Surge-tank dynamics

Continuity gives

\[A_s\dot h_s=Q_1-Q_2,\]

therefore

\[\boxed{\dot h_s=\frac{Q_1-Q_2}{A_s}}.\]

6. Downstream penstock dynamics

\[L_2\dot Q_2=gA_2\left(h_s-H_t-h_{f,2}(Q_2)\right),\]

with

\[h_{f,2}(Q_2)=K_2Q_2|Q_2|.\]

Hence

\[\boxed{\dot Q_2=\frac{gA_2}{L_2}\left(h_s-H_t-K_2Q_2|Q_2|\right)}.\]

The hydraulic state vector is therefore

\[x_h=\begin{bmatrix}Q_1 & h_s & Q_2\end{bmatrix}^{T}.\]

7. Turbine algebraic model

A simple nonlinear guide-vane/flow relation is

\[Q_2=C_dA_g(y_g)\sqrt{2gH_t}.\]

Equivalently,

\[\boxed{0=Q_2-C_dA_g(y_g)\sqrt{2gH_t}}.\]

The hydraulic power and mechanical power are

\[P_h=\rho gQ_2H_t,\]
\[\boxed{P_m=\eta_t(Q_2,H_t,\omega)\rho gQ_2H_t}.\]

The turbine torque is

\[\boxed{T_m=\frac{P_m}{\omega}}.\]

8. Shaft and rotor dynamics

The rotor-angle equation is

\[\boxed{\dot\delta=\omega-\omega_s}.\]

The rotational momentum balance is

\[J\dot\omega=T_m-T_e-D(\omega-\omega_s).\]

In power form,

\[\boxed{M\dot{\Delta\omega}=P_m-P_e-D\Delta\omega},\qquad \Delta\omega=\omega-\omega_s.\]

The mechanical state vector is

\[x_m=\begin{bmatrix}\delta & \omega\end{bmatrix}^{T}.\]

9. Generator algebraic model

If electromagnetic transients are neglected, a classical power-angle relation is

\[\boxed{P_e=\frac{EV}{X}\sin\delta}.\]

Equivalently,

\[0=P_e-\frac{EV}{X}\sin\delta.\]

The electromagnetic torque is

\[T_e=\frac{P_e}{\omega}.\]

10. Load and power balance

A static voltage- and frequency-dependent load can be written as

\[P_L=P_{L0}\left(\frac{V}{V_0}\right)^\alpha\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_0}\right)^\beta.\]

For a single-bus representation, active-power balance is

\[\boxed{0=P_e-P_L-P_{\mathrm{grid}}}.\]

11. Complete nonlinear DAE

The differential equations are summarized by

\[\boxed{\dot x=f(x,z,u,\theta)},\]

and the algebraic equations by

\[\boxed{0=g(x,z,u,\theta)}.\]

The complete system is therefore

\[\boxed{F(\dot x,x,z,u,\theta)=0}.\]

12. Explicit differential subsystem

\[\dot x=\begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{gA_1}{L_1}\left(H_r-h_s-K_1Q_1|Q_1|\right)\\[2mm] \dfrac{Q_1-Q_2}{A_s}\\[2mm] \dfrac{gA_2}{L_2}\left(h_s-H_t-K_2Q_2|Q_2|\right)\\[2mm] \omega-\omega_s\\[2mm] \dfrac{1}{J}\left(T_m-T_e-D(\omega-\omega_s)\right) \end{bmatrix}.\]

13. Algebraic subsystem

\[0=Q_2-C_dA_g(y_g)\sqrt{2gH_t},\]
\[0=P_h-\rho gQ_2H_t,\]
\[0=P_m-\eta_t\rho gQ_2H_t,\]
\[0=T_m-\frac{P_m}{\omega},\]
\[0=P_e-\frac{EV}{X}\sin\delta,\]
\[0=P_L-P_{L0}\left(\frac{V}{V_0}\right)^\alpha\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_0}\right)^\beta,\]
\[0=P_e-P_L-P_{\mathrm{grid}}.\]

The exact number of algebraic equations depends on the selected turbine, generator, network, and load detail.

14. Why this formulation is acausal

For a hydraulic connection, conservation and compatibility give

\[Q_1+Q_2=0,\qquad H_1=H_2.\]

For a rotational mechanical connection,

\[\tau_1+\tau_2=0,\qquad \omega_1=\omega_2.\]

For an electrical connection,

\[i_1+i_2=0,\qquad v_1=v_2.\]

These equations do not prescribe a fixed input-output direction. The distinction is

\[\text{causal: } y=\phi(u),\qquad \text{acausal: }F(y,u)=0.\]

15. Controller equations are conceptually separate

An isochronous PI governor may be written as

\[\dot\xi=\omega_{\mathrm{ref}}-\omega,\]
\[y_g=K_p(\omega_{\mathrm{ref}}-\omega)+K_i\xi.\]

Droop control may be written as

\[\Delta P_{\mathrm{ref}}=-\frac{1}{R}\Delta\omega.\]

These equations can be coupled to the nonlinear DAE without changing the meaning of physical acausality.

16. Equilibrium operating point

Let \((x^\star,z^\star,u^\star)\) be an operating point. It must satisfy

\[\boxed{f(x^\star,z^\star,u^\star,\theta)=0},\]
\[\boxed{g(x^\star,z^\star,u^\star,\theta)=0}.\]

At equilibrium,

\[\dot Q_1=\dot h_s=\dot Q_2=\dot\delta=\dot\omega=0.\]

17. Linearization of the DAE

Define perturbations

\[\Delta x=x-x^\star,\qquad \Delta z=z-z^\star,\qquad \Delta u=u-u^\star.\]

The first-order model is

\[\Delta\dot x=A_x\Delta x+A_z\Delta z+B\Delta u,\]
\[0=G_x\Delta x+G_z\Delta z+G_u\Delta u.\]

If \(G_z\) is nonsingular,

\[\Delta z=-G_z^{-1}G_x\Delta x-G_z^{-1}G_u\Delta u.\]

Substitution gives the reduced state-space model

\[\boxed{\Delta\dot x=A\Delta x+B_r\Delta u}.\]

18. Connection to FCR and LFC

The primary-control path is

\[\Delta\omega\rightarrow\text{governor}\rightarrow\Delta y_g\rightarrow\text{hydraulic system}\rightarrow\Delta P_m\rightarrow\text{shaft/generator}\rightarrow\Delta\omega.\]

A useful transfer function is

\[\boxed{G_{P_m\omega}(s)=\frac{\Delta P_m(s)}{\Delta\omega(s)}}.\]

Another useful plant transfer function is

\[G_{P_my_g}(s)=\frac{\Delta P_m(s)}{\Delta y_g(s)}.\]

19. Equation count

For this simplified model,

\[\boxed{3\text{ hydraulic differential equations}+2\text{ mechanical differential equations}=5\text{ differential states}}.\]

The number of algebraic equations is model-dependent and should not be fixed until the turbine, electrical network, load, and generator detail are fully specified.

20. Final model

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}\dot x&=f(x,z,u,\theta),\\0&=g(x,z,u,\theta).\end{aligned}}\]
Next step. Starting from an equilibrium \((x^\star,z^\star,u^\star)\), we can derive the Jacobians analytically, eliminate the algebraic perturbations, and obtain a reduced linear model for governor, droop, FCR, and AGC studies.
